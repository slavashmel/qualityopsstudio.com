# Site And Offer Growth Plan

This note turns the founder evidence map into practical website, content, and offer improvements for QualityOps Studio.

## Strategic Read

The current site already has the right core: QualityOps Studio is not a testing vendor, but a consulting practice for release confidence, regression speed, automation signal, ownership, and QA operating models.

The next improvement is to make the offer feel more tangible to CTOs and VP Engineering leaders. They should not only understand the promise; they should see the shape of the work, the artifacts they will get, and the management decisions it helps them make.

## Best Differentiation

Use this wedge:

> QualityOps Studio builds the quality operating system for teams where QA, automation, CI/CD, Jira, ownership, and AI workflows have become one connected delivery problem.

Why this is stronger than generic QA consulting:

- It includes technical inspection, not just advisory.
- It includes people and operating model, not just automation.
- It is evidence-backed by 10h -> 30m regression, about 50% automation cost reduction, 30+ QA organization scale, onboarding improvement, Jira dependency automation, and AI-assisted workflows.
- It fits the current market where AI creates more output, but teams still need quality infrastructure and release discipline.

## Website Improvements

### 1. Add a "What We Diagnose" Visual

Add an infographic near the top, after proof points or before offers.

Concept: a quality system map with six nodes:

- Requirements signal
- Jira / dependency flow
- Regression model
- Automation signal
- CI/CD and environments
- Ownership and release decision

The visual should show that QualityOps Studio reviews the whole system, not isolated testing tasks.

Suggested headline:

> Quality failures rarely come from one test suite. They come from the system around it.

### 2. Add Mini Case Study Cards

Add a section with three compact, anonymized case cards:

- 10-hour regression to 30-minute release signal.
- Around 50% automation cost reduction.
- Cross-functional onboarding that saved 1-2 weeks.

Each card should use the same structure:

- Symptom
- Intervention
- Result
- Why it mattered to leadership

This would make the current proof band more believable and memorable.

### 3. Rename "Method" Into Buyer Language

Current "Method" is good, but a buyer may not immediately know what they receive.

Alternative section title:

> From hidden quality risk to an operating roadmap.

Suggested steps:

1. Map how quality actually moves.
2. Find the constraints that slow releases.
3. Design the operating model.
4. Transfer ownership to the team.

### 4. Add a Diagnostic Deliverables Preview

The strongest entry offer is Quality System Diagnostic, so the site should show a concrete preview.

Add a dark or light operational panel with:

- Current-state quality map.
- Bottleneck and risk register.
- Regression and automation signal review.
- Ownership and release-readiness model.
- 30/60/90-day roadmap.
- Executive workshop.

This makes the diagnostic feel buyable.

### 5. Add a "Not For Everyone" Filter

This can make the offer feel more premium.

Good fit:

- Scaling engineering team.
- Regression, automation, or release confidence is already painful.
- Leadership wants ownership, not only more testers.
- Team is ready to change workflow, not just buy hours.

Bad fit:

- Need only manual test execution.
- Need cheap outsourced QA capacity.
- Want automation scripts without process ownership.

### 6. Add Personal Proof Without Making It A CV

The founder section can become sharper:

- Head of QA, 20+ years in IT.
- Built and led 30+ QA organization.
- Worked across telecom, fintech, trading, enterprise, infrastructure, and product teams.
- Public Russian-language talks on fast E2E, regression reduction, and onboarding.

Keep it short. The site sells a business outcome, not a resume.

## Possible New Offer

### AI-Assisted QA Workflow Review

Format: 1-2 weeks.

Buyer problem:

- teams use AI for test cases, requirements, bug analysis, or automation support;
- leadership is unsure whether this improves quality or just creates more low-trust artifacts.

Deliverables:

- AI workflow inventory;
- risk and data-safety review;
- prompt/process templates;
- Jira/Confluence/TMS integration recommendations;
- pilot workflow with evaluation criteria.

This should be secondary for now, not the main offer, but it is a strong market-facing wedge.
